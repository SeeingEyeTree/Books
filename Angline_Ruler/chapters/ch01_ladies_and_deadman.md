# Chapter 1: Ladies and Deadman

> Original rough draft (author's), unedited. Needs conversion to first person present and an ending; see knowledge/continuity.md.

"You shot him. You shot him! Are you insane?"

Bethany pressed herself against the lab bench, knocking over a tray of sample vials. She was staring at me, then at Alphonseo — who was now contemplating his life choices on the floor — then back at me.

I replaced the safety on the pistol I technically shouldn't have been allowed in the building with, and considered the question seriously. Why would someone speak if their words held no meaning?

"No," I said. "If anything, I am the corrective force applied to a world that has drifted from sanity." I paused. "It sounds so crude if you say I shot him. I like to say he died of something adjacent to natural causes."

"Adjacent to—" Bethany stopped. Blinked. "What does that mean?"

"You see, your blood contains iron. That iron moves through your heart constantly. A bullet also contains iron. So when a bullet passes through your heart it is simply a great quantity of iron passing into and through the heart at once." I gave the explanation for what a gunshot wound really was. "Adjacent to natural causes. One might even say a heart attack." Many people might disagree with my framing but many people are wrong.

I was met with silence. Alphonseo decided then to pipe up with some of his last time alive and said "A thousand curses upon you." Accomplishing absolutely nothing, but what is a dying man to do. I kicked him for good measure.

“Don’t you hate it when they don’t die instantly.” If they had just let me bring my suit he would have been given a painless death.

Bethany blinked again. Twice. She was probably sympathizing with how people tend to live a little longer than you hoped. 

"But why? Why would you kill him? He only ever helped you!" Bethany seemed to be trying to find reason in a world that so often lacked it. I actually did have a reason this time.

"Well he would have tried to stop me. That is illegal so he must be stopped in turn." I declared this law in the hopes that she didn’t try to break it. People who did only even ended up one way. The flame of my ambition never wavered and I would allow none to put it out.

Bethany looked quite hesitant to ask, but curiosity is one of the strongest motivators. "Stopped you from doing what exactly?"

"I am glad you asked. Allow me to Monologue." I spread my hands out while saying this for dramatic effect. I was waiting for someone to ask. I always came prepared. I pulled out the remote for the projector I insisted we had installed in this room. My machinations run deep.

The lab was not a small room. It had not been a small room before Angline had quietly requisitioned two of the adjacent offices over the course of a month, knocking through the walls during a long weekend while Alphonseo was at a conference in Helsinki. It was now one long irregular space that smelled of solvent and ambition, lined with equipment that was technically the property of the building and practically the property of Angline Ruler. A projector screen hung at the far end, installed at her insistence during a facilities request that had described it as "essential for collaborative science." The screen was motorized. The hum it made while descending was self-important in a way Angline found deeply appropriate.

It was a little bit awkward waiting for the screen to come down and the projector to warm up while standing over the now thankfully dead body of my former boss. He was hanging in there and thrashing around, but people tended to have a hard time getting oxygen to their body when more of their blood was outside than in. I was just waited patientlyt, that and a few kicks.

"Shouldn't you be running away or panicking? You seem to be taking this awfully well."

Bethany really thought about it for a second. "...I'm too tired to care."

"You have poor survival instincts. Exactly what we are looking for in new hires. An opening was just created."

The projector finally warmed up and her slides popped up with a cheerful click. The title slide filled the wall. Angline produced the clicker from her coat pocket — the coat she wore at all times, indoors and out, which had more pockets than any coat had a right to — and pointed it at the screen with the energy of someone who had been waiting a very long time to do this.

---

LADIES AND DEADMAN

*presented by ANGLINE RULER*

---

"Ladies and Deadman," she began, bouncing slightly on her heels. "I am Angline Ruler. Today we will be covering: Our Plans for the New World. Please save any questions for the end."

She advanced the slide.

"While demolishing an orphanage we found the door to a new world." She said this the way someone else might say *while reorganizing the supply closet.* "So far you have struggled to open that door. I found the key. I intend to advance my goals in this new world." She pointed her clicker at a photograph of a shimmering circular anomaly that had been captured in sub-basement four, seventeen days ago, and filed under 'construction rubble' in the official report, because Alphonseo had told her not to make a scene.

Alphonseo was no longer in a position to tell her anything.

"History teaches us one reliable method of wealth accumulation." Next slide. The word THEFT appeared in a large sans-serif font, centered, as if it were a motivational poster. "The Spanish requisitioned gold from the people of South America. The British brought civilization to many a nation. The Americans found vast swaths of empty land." She clicked again, grinning. "We hope to follow in the footsteps of these great — if morally instructive — examples. I intend to do so with at least fifty percent more human rights."

"We will be the Aristocracy." She let it sit in the room for a moment. It sat there, getting comfortable. "The best should rule and I am the best the world has ever seen." This was delivered not as a boast but as a statement of observable fact, the way one might note a temperature reading. "Join me and we will rule this planet — or see it burnt to ashes before us." She paused. "I mean two people could do twice as much work as one. Well, given one of them is me, maybe only 1.5 times as much. But still a large fraction."

She needed Bethany, was the thing. Not desperately. Not the way you need air or water. More the way you need a second person to move a large piece of furniture — technically possible alone, just more annoying.

---

Bethany put her hands on the bench behind her and leaned. She had, in the last eight minutes, watched her superior shot, sat through a PowerPoint presentation that began with a slide reading "Ladies and Deadman," and been offered what appeared to be a sincere if unconventional job offer. She was, as she had said, too tired to care. But she was also a scientist, and scientists asked questions.

"If I don't join you," she said, "what happens to me?"

Angline brightened. The brightness was immediate and total, like a lamp switched on. "I would, as all great villains do when faced with a refusal, arrange for your rather theatrical demise. I would lower you very slowly into a pit of lava."

Bethany stared at her. Then she turned and looked through the internal window into the next room — the one Angline had also knocked through to, during a separate long weekend, for reasons that had at the time been described in the facilities form as "ventilation improvements."

The next room contained a large pool. The pool contained lava.

"So *that* is why you had us install a pool of lava," Bethany said. "You know how much it costs to make and maintain that. All so you could make a threat when you already had a gun."

"Eh, if no one questions you, you can get away with murder." Angline gestured toward Alphonseo, who was running out of thrashing. "But you would be fine if I lowered you into the lava anyway. You would naturally be saved right before you reached the bottom by a dashing young hero who has come to thwart my most devious plans."

"Did you arrange for a dashing young hero?"

"Well — strictly speaking, no." She made a gesture that communicated this was a technicality. "But that is simply how the world works. The architecture of narrative demands it." Two thumbs up. "So no need to worry."

Bethany looked at the body of her former boss, at the projector still humming on slide seven — a rough hand-drawn diagram titled THE PLAN, consisting mostly of arrows and a single bullet point reading WIN — and at the woman who had done all of this with the energy of an evil golden retriever.

"So," she said, "you seem to have been light on the details. What exactly is your plan?"

"Well I must monologue responsibly — I can't reveal everything until you join the fold. What I can say is that I do have two flamethrowers."

A beat.

"Nothing more needs to be said," Bethany said. "Great plan, Ms. Ruler. Great plan."

"So you're joining the team?"

"Well I would rather die of actual natural causes instead of something against them. The human body generates heat. Being thrown in lava with just a lot of heat is what I fear you will say gleefully killing me."

"See, you get it." Angline was already moving, pocketing the clicker, pulling out her phone. "You made the only choice you could. Let me just call Billy the APC and we can be on our way."

I always played with a stacked deck. Billy was my Ace.

The loading bay in sub-basement two was not somewhere Alphonseo had ever visited, which had made stocking it considerably easier. It smelled of diesel and welded steel and the particular charged-air quality of something that had a great deal of power stored in it and was waiting to use it. Billy sat at the center of it, broad and blunt-nosed and painted the flat grey-green of something trying very hard not to be noticed despite being enormous. He had a 35mm auto cannon. Two missile launchers and a "directed energy weapon" powered by a 1MW reactor Angline had personally designed during a work trip to Geneva, in a hotel room, in three days, because she had been bored. He also had seventeen different drones for recon and to project power, each slotted into their housings along his flanks like the world's most aggressive advent calendar.

She did not intend to face the New World with nothing but the clothes on her back.

Food, water, recon tools, the basic boring calculus of survival — yes, fine, she had those too, packed sensibly into the storage bays. But more importantly she had more C4 than most small nations, which was a sentence she had worked very hard to be able to say. Working for the military and being handed a blank check really did have its perks. Her only genuine regret in life, at this stage, was that she hadn't been able to source a trailer. She intended to find out whether all problems could be solved with sufficient application of C4. She was betting yes, with the confidence of someone who was always right.

Bethany walked into the loading bay, looked at Billy, and was briefly speechless.

"Is that a directed energy weapon?"

"1 MW." Angline said it the way someone else might brag about a nice car.

"Where did you get a 1 MW reactor."

"I made it."

"When?"

"Geneva. I was bored."

Bethany looked at Billy for another long moment. Then she looked at Angline. Then back at Billy. The arithmetic she was doing was clearly not going well for any version of her life choices.

"Right," she said. "Let's go burn down a new world."

"That is the spirit." Angline vaulted into the passenger seat with the agility of someone who had practiced this. "Though I prefer *conquer.* Burning is a last resort. Or a first resort. I haven't fully decided."

The portal in sub-basement four pulsed, indifferent to the armored vehicle now rolling toward it at speed.
