# Technology and Industry

## Billy (the APC)

Amphibious armored vehicle, flat grey-green, broad and blunt-nosed. Angline's "Ace." Originally equipped with:
- 35mm autocannon (later modified to ~10,000 rounds/minute)
- Two missile launchers
- A "directed energy weapon"
- A 1 MW D-T fusion reactor, designed by Angline in a Geneva hotel room in three days ("I was bored")
- 17 drones (recon/power projection), later supplemented by Brask's works
- Radar — the one sensor nothing in the Green has ever needed to hide from (light-bending and heat-masking predators/Shades are invisible to camera and thermal, but not radar)
- C4, "more than most small nations"
- Keeps ~10 g of tritium reserve so it never runs dry mid-mission

## Mote-based weapons (Angline's own, Ch9+)

- **Push-rune glove:** steel palm plate with a milled push rune, thumb-operated sprung slide moves a mote into intake range. ~25 kJ push per mote on Earth (enough to throw a soldier ~4 m); binds its reaction into the floor through her boots (see magic_system.md, force physics) so she isn't thrown back.
- **Heat discs:** steel pucks with a spiral rune, pin holds a mote off until pulled; ~2 seconds of intense heat on release.
- Used non-lethally in the Ch9 confrontation — nobody killed; soldiers thrown, disarmed, rifles bent.

## Fusion and tritium

- **The problem:** Earth's total civilian tritium supply (a few tens of kg) is far below what's needed to run a grid-scale fusion buildout (~200 kg/year for ~1.2 GW electric), and every gram on Earth is tracked/inspected — buying it is impossible.
- **The solution — breeding:** surround the reactor with a lithium(-lead) blanket. Fusion neutrons convert lithium into new tritium (breeding ratio ~1.15: burn 100 atoms, breed back 115). Net tritium inventory grows ~7%/day, doubling every ~9-10 days.
- **Starting stock:** Billy's onboard 40 g (Angline keeps 30 g for the seed reactor, Billy keeps 10 g reserve). ~4 months from 30 g to 200 kg (grid scale).
- **Lithium source:** the salt pan found while prospecting (Ch11). **Lead** (for the blanket) comes as a byproduct of Pinnacle's silver-lead ore (already being mined for the silver-arbitrage trade).
- **Named reactors** (a running joke — always "why nobody questions [X]" or self-deprecating):
  - ***Eleven Months*** — seed reactor by the Sable Gate, named for how long her own reactor design sat with a government committee before Ch1.
  - ***Personal Use*** — first full-size reactor (300 MW), named for the spy satellite the government refused her.
  - ***Bielefeld*** — second full-size reactor, on the coast plateau ("Bielefeld doesn't exist, and neither, officially, does this" — referencing the German conspiracy joke that the city of Bielefeld doesn't exist).

## Steel and heavy industry

- **The Red Ridge mill (Ch11):** shallow hematite ridge, 60 km west of Pinnacle, leased from Ochre (99 years, 1-in-20 royalty). Blast furnace uses husk-wood charcoal as reductant, heat-rune-lined furnace walls (motes → heat at near 100% efficiency, cheapest effect), oxygen from water electrolysis, rune-motor rolling mill. Employs ~900 lower-city workers. ~900+ t/day by end of Ch11, targeting 2,000 t/day. Beams stamped "made by Ruler."
- **Copper mill:** smaller operation at the copper deposit 110 km NW of Pinnacle.
- **CNC machining (should be understood as existing from around Ch11, the steel mill era — retroactive addition, not yet written into the chapter text):** a control loop plus G-code-equivalent toolpaths, with electric or rune-driven axes. Lets Angline mill precision rune plates, turbine parts, and tooling *locally* instead of importing precision parts through a door. This is what later scales into Brask's rune works (Ch15): CNC-milled megalith and production runes to 0.01 mm precision, in bronze (heat) and dense Red Ridge stone (push).

## Vertical farms

- **Concept:** the Houses farm horizontally because light is their most expensive mote-effect (~0.1% efficient). Angline's LEDs make light cheap, so she farms *vertically* — 30 floors on land licensed by ground area only (a loophole Houses haven't closed).
- **Ecosystem loop:** husk-wood (drained/dead stalks, free from the Houses) → fungus (grown in the dark, no light needed) → black soldier fly larvae (eat spent fungus substrate) → droppings fertilize duckweed/bamboo shoots → waste and leftovers burn in the boiler → boiler lights the floors. Nothing wasted; everything eventually dies in front of a crystal.
- **Research loop (Ch7):** 100 automated growth chambers, each with sensors + a husk to measure yield, run by an optimizer maximizing motes/m² of ground/day. Bethany runs it (ex-pharma high-throughput screening background). Found the yeast/living-tissue insight (see magic_system.md).
- **Construction (Ch8 breakthrough):** rune lifters with real feedback control (read state 500x/sec, correct for distance/drift/speed, plus gravity feedforward) hold beams to ±2 mm; swarms of ~20 lifters plus heat-rune welding crawlers with thermocouple feedback plus hourly drone LiDAR checks build a floor every 2-3 days with zero Angline involvement. Later "keel"-motif lifters (Ch14) are half the weight, build a floor in 1.5 days.
- **Output at end of Ch10:** Farm 1 (Verdane, 30 floors) 61,000/day; Farm 2 (Sable) 48,000/day; Farm 3 (Ochre, 12 floors) 17,000/day. Total 126,000/day.
- **Farm handover (Ch13):** the first three farms (Verdane's, Sable's, Ochre's) pass to those Houses, running, fully trained, as the price of the rail right-of-way. Real cost: their output, and the end of Angline's vertical-farm monopoly (House copies expected within ~3-5 years).
- **Coastal farms (from Ch13):** built on Ruler's Reach, kelp-fed (solves the feed bottleneck locally), staffed by trained coast workers. First coastal farm: 30 floors, 40,000 motes/day.
- **The farm race (Ch14):** Angline thinks in the second derivative — "farms per week, per week." Build rate starts at 1 farm/12 days, target: double every ~10 weeks, ~150 farms in year one. Bottleneck sequence found and pushed: steel → power → feed → **people** (the hard one — ~60 staff/farm × ~150 farms ≈ 9,000 workers, the whole coast population). Robots rejected as not worth it (harvest floors are too many varied tasks with no training data). Solution: Bethany's "teachers per week, per week" — paid 6-week schools where each cohort's best graduates teach the next, plus hiring from the whole coast, rail towns, and even Pinnacle's lower cities (poaching House labor).

## Rail

- **Chosen over canal:** barges are cheapest per tonne-km but a 400 km canal (30 m wide, 4 m deep, ~48M m³, plus locks) would take ~11 years with current-generation lifters. Rail: single track, passing loops every 20 km, 60 kg/m rail, ~48,000 t of Red Ridge steel (~50 days of mill output).
- **Construction:** starts in the unclaimed wild 60 km beyond House ward-posts (no permission needed), self-laying train with rune lifters + welding crawlers, ~3 km/day. First spike: silver, stamped "made by Ruler."
- **Crossing House land (Ch13):** required the Right-of-Way deal — see current_state.md for terms. Three track-laying trains work simultaneously (coast, Red Ridge, middle) once the deal is signed; line completes in ~6 weeks.
- **Power:** electrified from fusion as tritium reaches grid scale; power lines share the rail corridor. First locomotive (pre-electrification): secondhand diesel-electric from Earth, later retired to shunting duty ("spinning magnets with extra steps").

## Drones and demonstration hardware (Ch15)

- **Brask's works** built 4,000 dinner-plate-sized drones for the Brussels demonstration: 4 rotors, a small core, a bronze disc milled with a ring of light-bending runes (the "Shade trick," scaled up and pointed at a city). Individually near-undetectable; in formation (a 12 km shell above 4,000 m) bend light around an entire capital for a timed interval.
- **Megalith stone runes** (car-sized, CNC-milled): used for door-opening/sealing rings (12-24 stones depending on job) and are the same class of hardware used for House megaliths (though Angline's are ferrofluid-refined designs cast in optimal material, built faster than any House could match).
