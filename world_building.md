# World Building

## The two worlds

- **The Green:** The world on the far side of the portal. Same planet shape, size, gravity, day length, and latitude/longitude grid as Earth (confirmed by sun angle, compass, and gravity measurements, Ch6/9).
- **One-to-one mapping:** Every point on Earth has a twin location in the Green at the same coordinates. A door opened at a given Earth location opens at the matching Green location, and vice versa. This is how new doors are planned (the Fernholt bunker → House Sable's fields; the salt mine → the cliffs above Ruler's Reach; Brussels → an unfarmed marsh).
- **No radio on the Green.** Zero EM signals detected on arrival (Ch2). Confirms lower tech level, and later means radar is Angline's unique sensory edge (nothing there has ever needed to hide from radio; see magic_system.md).
- **Earth tax:** All magical effects are roughly 10x weaker on Earth than on the Green (measured in Ch6). See magic_system.md for numbers.

## Geography of the Green (as explored)

- **The Green (continent):** A vast landmass, most of it under House-farmed bamboo ("the Green" as both the farmland and the world's nickname). Roughly the size of a large European country from Pinnacle's cluster alone; the full continent is much larger.
- **Pinnacle:** ~3 km wide walled city, ringed by bare earth then bamboo fields. Angline and Bethany's arrival point (Ch2-3) and first city (Ch4+). Seat of Houses Verdane, Sable, and Ochre.
- **The Red Ridge:** Shallow hematite deposit, 60 km west of Pinnacle. Leased from Ochre, 99 years, 1 in 20 of output. Site of Angline's steel mill (~900+ t/day) and the CNC/rune works infrastructure that later moves to the coast.
- **Copper deposit:** 110 km northwest of Pinnacle.
- **Lithium salt pan:** Far south of Pinnacle; feeds the tritium-breeding blanket.
- **Wild bamboo:** Beyond House ward-posts, unfarmed, dangerous (invisible predators, radar-visible only).
- **The mountains to the south:** Steep, unfarmed, dangerous wildlife. Twin of an Alpine rail tunnel (thin place #2).
- **The Brine (ocean):** ~400 km east of Pinnacle. Kelp forests, dense fish shoals, scattered fishing villages, no House claims (harvest law stops at the waterline).
- **Ruler's Reach:** Deep, cliff-guarded bay, ~400 km east of Pinnacle / the Fernholt bunker area, 40 km south of the nearest fishing village. Angline's coastal base. Home of Shingle, the growing coastal town, the coastal farms, the rail terminus, and the sealed Reach door site.
- **Shingle:** Fishing village at Ruler's Reach, 312 people originally, now the core of a town of 1,460+ (Ch13) and growing.
- **The Green twin of Brussels:** Unfarmed marsh, ~400 km NW of the coast.
- **Tessary:** A city with its own Houses, far from Pinnacle; twin of the "Quiet Room" thin place. Angline sealed a thin place there covertly, without permission or contact.
- **41 cities** total across the Green (per the cartographer's map, Ch5), each with its own 2-3 Houses (~100 Houses total, per Brask).

## Pinnacle

- **Layout:** ~3 km across. Ringed by bare earth then bamboo. Towers packed tight, connected by bridges at many heights. Built vertically because every square meter of ground is farmland.
- **Class by altitude:** Ground level ("the shaft," lower city) is dark and poor; nobody there can afford light. Upper "Leaf bridges" burn crystal light even in daylight. The rich live in the light.
- **The Stake Hall:** Where the three Houses meet under the Compact. Ground level, city center, black stone, one round room, round table under a hole in the roof (no House sits higher). Three doors, one per House; no fourth door for outsiders.
- **Flame law:** Open flame banned above a certain height (Pinnacle: above the third bridge) due to poor ventilation in the dense wooden upper tiers.

### The three Houses of Pinnacle

- **House Verdane:** The Lady. Angline's first charter (Ch7). Farm One (now theirs). Great crystal in the Lamp Hall, ~400 years old, crowned by a megalith rune.
- **House Sable:** The Lord. Second charter. The Sable Gate (sealed Ch15) was in Sable's field. Farm Two (now theirs).
- **House Ochre:** The Lady. Third charter. Leases the Red Ridge. Farm Three (now theirs), 8-floor tower under construction.

### Money and economy

- **Motes** are the currency and the fuel (see magic_system.md for physics). Spent crystals are "husks."
- **Entry to Pinnacle:** 2 motes/person (historical).
- **Historical prices (pre-Angline):** porridge/loaf/skewer 1 mote each (day's food ~3 motes); live/dressed hen 4 motes; cart of produce 12 motes.
- **Household budget (family of 4/week, pre-Angline):** food 84, water 5, lifting 3, cooking heat 4, light 1-2, plus House tithe. Light is the most expensive mote effect (~20 min per mote).
- **The Houses' power:** They own the bamboo, the crystals, and therefore the right to make money (harvest law — see magic_system.md). Collectors (gray coats, bamboo-leaf crest) tithe stall owners' motes into a House crystal.

## History of the Green (codex — reveal gradually)

| Era | Rough date | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Wild Age | Before ~3,000 years ago | Small bands follow herds, camp at natural "death-places" (wild crystal sites). Motes rare and precious. |
| The First Fields | ~3,000 years ago | Agriculture is doubly good: food *and* motes from every harvest. Farming peoples spread fast, push the wild back. |
| The Age of Seats | ~2,000-800 years ago | Farming towns plant mother crystals, build fortified seats. Faction strength tracks land held. Constant war. First megalith runes raised over seats. |
| The Red Harvest | ~800-600 years ago | Warlords kill captives for motes — a terrible crop, but destroys enemy labor in war. (Brask's hint: "someone did your sum.") |
| Ashfall | ~550 years ago | A besieged seat's great crystal cracks during an assault. The city of Carrow and both armies vanish. Carrow Mere (round crater lake) marks the spot. Taught to every House child. |
| The Compact of Stakes | ~500 years ago | Surviving factions agree: only Houses may harvest (harvest law), captives may not be harvested, seats are never stormed. War continues, but only over fields. |
| The Age of Houses | ~500 years ago-now | Stable House system. Cities build vertically to spare farmland. Field ecosystems refined over centuries. Every House seat crowned by a megalith rune. |

## How war works in the Green

- **Shades:** Elite casters who fight wars. Carry large amounts of motes, stay cool, push things away, bend light around themselves (nearly invisible). Can put a pebble through a body at range, or wrap an unshielded opponent in plasma.
- **Surprise wins:** Kill a Shade by surprise, take their carried motes. Take territory, get its land and storage.
- **Why it doesn't snowball (deliberate worldbuilding to avoid "more motes = automatic win"):**
  - **Focus limit:** Casting spends the caster's attention, not just the mote. A Shade with a million motes can't spend faster than one with ten thousand. Takes a decade to train a Shade.
  - **Weight:** A mote weighs ~5 g; 100,000 motes = half a tonne. Raids are short, campaigns run dry. (Cores break this brake — see magic_system.md.)
  - **Home ground:** Defenders fight beside field crystals and always-on ward-runes needing no focus. Every seat has a megalith rune. Attackers can only carry what they can carry.
  - **Seats are bombs:** Great crystals are nuclear-scale reservoirs (see Ashfall). Storming a seat risks destroying everything. Mutual deterrence.
  - **Land needs loyal hands:** Captured fields only print motes if farmers keep harvesting. Conquest yields less than it looks.
  - **Loot breeds desertion:** A Shade who scores a fortune often just leaves.
  - **The Compact:** 500 years of custom. War is fought over fields only, by raids and challenges, ending in treaties.
  - **What Angline is quietly breaking:** rune machines with controllers (no focus needed) and cores (100,000 motes in a backpack) remove the focus and weight brakes. The Lord of Sable is starting to sense this.

## The fields: a symphony of death

The Houses' fields are engineered ecosystems, not bamboo monocultures, refined over centuries to maximize motes per hectare per day:

- **Canopy:** giant bamboo, fast growth, shades layers below.
- **Understory:** shade crops between the stalks.
- **Grazers:** domesticated animals eating the understory, harvested on schedule (animals >> plants in motes/kg).
- **Fungus:** breaks down cut stalks and dead matter, harvested in turn.
- **Mother crystals:** at the heart of each field section; every death feeds one.
- Harvests are timed by season and generation — old, deep agricultural science, guarded like a trade secret.
- **Ward-posts** at field edges: crystal-fed runes keeping wild predators out.

**Implication:** the Houses are not naive about how motes are made. Angline's edge is lab speed (research loop vs. generations), stacking floors under cheap LED light, and fusion power — she re-derives in months what they learned over centuries, then surpasses it.

## Wildlife

- Animals use motes exactly as humans do — nothing about magic is human-specific.
- **Wild crystals** form naturally at "death-places" (herd crossings, predator dens, reefs, flood plains).
- **Dangerous fauna:** predators near wild crystals learn to bend light, push, and chill themselves to hide heat. Farmland has been hunted clear of them for centuries (why Angline met none early on). Mountains, deep forest, and the sea have not been cleared.
- **The Glass:** ~30 m, ~20 t sea predator that bends light, living near Ruler's Reach. Has kept Houses off this coast for 600 years (a Sable collector was eaten a generation ago). Angline chose not to kill it (~16,000 motes to do so) — it's free coastal defense.
- **The invisible predator (Ch12):** horse-sized, bends light, hides heat; only visible on radar. Circled Angline's camp in the wild 60 km beyond the ward-posts.

## The coast / Ruler's Reach

- **Shingle:** original fishing village, 312 people, 41 driftwood-and-stone huts, 11 boats. Coast dialect. Pre-Angline problems: smoke-filled huts (poor draft), cholera-like summer deaths from a goat-fouled stream, income of only 30-40 motes/month from a wild sea-stack crystal.
- **The deal:** not charity — Shingle works for Angline (kelp lines, piloting, guiding) at Pinnacle wage rates, in exchange for clean water, flues, lamps, boat motors, radar, and the Glass-ward. Cost ~4,000 motes setup, ~3,000/year — less than an hour of farm output.
- **Why (Angline's stated logic):** local knowledge, labor, a recruitment base along the coast (~30 villages, ~9,000 people), and legitimacy (land is only held with loyal hands on it).
- **"The Ruler":** what Shingle calls her, as a title.
- **Growth:** by Ch13, 1,460 people in 412 container cabins, laid out by Bethany and Marit along kinship lines (Kettle Point/Low Sand are cousins; Grayrock/Harrow feud over a net; Tern Hollow brought cholera).
- **Kelp:** grows fast in the bay, feeds the yeast floors, solves the "feed" bottleneck for coastal farms.
- **Rail:** chosen over canal (canal would take ~11 years with 2026-era lifters). Single track, passing loops, built from the Red Ridge's steel. Starts in the unclaimed wild 60 km, later crosses House land under the Right-of-Way deal (Ch13). Electrified from fusion power as tritium reaches grid scale.

## Earth locations

- **The lab:** Original site, sub-basement four (portal/scar site, sealed Ch15), sub-basement two (loading bay).
- **Fernholt Data Storage:** decommissioned Cold War bunker, 22.4 km from the lab (bearing 051°), bought via shell companies 3 years before Ch9. Housed the Sable Gate (sealed and emptied Ch15).
- **Kessel Mineral Archive:** shell company holding the salt mine (closed 1998, 200 m deep) that hosted the Reach door — sealed and emptied Ch15.
- **Three natural thin places (all sealed Ch15):**
  1. Flooded quarry, Ardennes, Belgium (Green twin: wild bamboo 300 km SW of Pinnacle).
  2. Disused 1911 Alpine rail tunnel, a spot permanently 6°C colder (Green twin: steep southern mountains).
  3. "The Quiet Room," basement of a psychiatric hospital closed 1987, 300 km north (Green twin: a House field outside Tessary).
- **Brussels:** site of the Ch15 demonstration (the "veil"). Twin: unfarmed marsh.
