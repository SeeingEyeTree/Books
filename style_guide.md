# Style Guide

## Premise

A self-declared perfect genius engineer murders her boss, recruits a too-tired coworker, and drives an armored vehicle through a portal into another world to build an empire. It is a comedy played completely straight by its narrator, and it slowly becomes a story about what a person like that builds, and who she brings with her.

## Point of view and tense

- **First person, present tense**, throughout.
- **Main narrator:** Angline. Sections headed `### Angline`.
- **Second narrator:** Bethany, from Ch13 on. Sections headed `### Bethany`. Her voice is tired, dry, doubting, and kinder than Angline's. She does not make tables.
- **Interludes:** Ch5 has one interlude from Oren's POV. Use sparingly, if at all.
- **Ch1 and Ch2** are original drafts in past tense with some third-person drift; they need converting (see continuity.md).
- **Locals' dialogue** (speech from the Green, via Billy's translator) is in *italics* without quotation marks. Earth speech uses normal quotation marks.

## Voice and humor

- **The engine:** Angline states monstrous or absurd things as calm, pedantic fact. The narration never winks. Bethany (and others) supply the deadpan reaction.
- **Rhythm:** Short declarative punchlines after longer setups. "A beat." One-word replies.
- **Engineer's brain:** Angline thinks in units, orders of magnitude, bottlenecks, and derivatives. Numbers should be real and roughly right. She hates numbers without units.
- **Stakes are real.** People die, get burned, get sick. The narrator treats it as an inconvenience; the story does not.
- **Villain self-awareness:** Angline knows she's a villain, loves the genre, and expects narrative conventions (monologues, heroes, lava pits) to hold.
- **Good deeds are utilitarian,** with a wink. She helps people because it's the optimal path, and always has a table to prove it. The one thing she doesn't put in a column is the tell.

## Author preferences (from feedback)

- **Pacing:** Slower is better. Fewer events per chapter, longer scenes. Early chapters felt rushed.
- **Not everything works out.** Real costs, real losses (e.g. Nkemelu leaving, the farm handover).
- **Small cast.** Two main characters (Angline, Bethany), a few strong supporting ones. Don't add characters casually.
- **Hard-ish science.** Magic can be hand-wavy about *why*, but strict about *what*: conservation of energy and momentum, attenuation, efficiencies.
- **Avoid forced symbolism.** A touching detail works once; repeated, it feels forced (the kitchen clock).
- **The Houses are not naive.** They are sophisticated, centuries-old institutions. They threaten politely and credibly.

## Running jokes and motifs

Reuse sparingly; each lands harder with space between callbacks.

- **"Adjacent to natural causes."** Pedantic reframing of violence.
- **Monologuing responsibly.** "Allow me to monologue." Withholding the plan until people "join the fold." Slide decks: *Ladies and Deadman*, *Lady and APC*, *Lady and Ledger*.
- **Spinning magnets.** Humanity's true purpose. Solar is heresy. Diesel-electric is "spinning magnets with extra steps."
- **"Great plan, Ms. Ruler. Great plan."** Bethany's line.
- **"Don't make it weird."** Angline, whenever someone notices she did something kind.
- **"Doctor Director."** Angline's name for Bethany.
- **The ledger.** Debts and credit columns; people get crossed off.
- **Stacked deck.** Billy was her Ace; the bunker was her King.
- **Vanity.** The badge photo vs. the portrait. "It takes effort to look effortlessly beautiful." Her hair.
- **Nobody questions Helsinki** (Alphonseo's cover story). Reactor names are running jokes: *Eleven Months*, *Personal Use*, *Bielefeld*.
- **Load-bearing belief.** People who think she answers to them are load-bearing walls; don't knock them out until the new structure is up.
- **Facilities forms.** Absurd renovations disguised as "ventilation improvements."
- **Theft as history's wealth method,** "with at least fifty percent more human rights."
- **Worthy opponent.** Angline is disappointed when no one is dangerous, and delighted when someone is.
- **"What do you want?"** The only question that matters in any negotiation.
